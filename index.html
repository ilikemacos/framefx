New in 1.4.1 Beta8

Smarter Preset Assistant. A new AI model that's better at follow-ups like "what about 4K", understands number words like "sixty", and handles new requests like "fans are too loud" and "kill frame gen".

CSR Flux V2 stays the frame generation model.

Windows only. The ROG Ally / Ally X edition stays on Beta4. Beta8 includes everything in Beta7:

Preset Assistant chat (AI, experimental). Describe your game, then keep chatting to adjust the suggested settings. Press Apply to save them as that game's profile, or Undo to go back. Works offline.

Clearer anti-cheat notes. Elden Ring is now flagged as using Easy Anti-Cheat only when playing online.

CSR Flux V2 stays the frame generation model.

Beta7 includes everything in Beta6:

Preset Assistant (AI, experimental). Describe your game and what you want, and get suggested settings for it. Check the suggestion, then press Apply to save it as that game's profile. Works offline.

CSR Flux V2. The sharper frame generation model from Beta5 stays in.

Beta6 includes everything in Beta5:

Sharper CSR Flux BETA. CSR Flux BETA has a new, sharper model at 2x/3x/4x.

Beta5 includes everything in Beta4:

CSR Flux is now CSR Flux BETA. The AI frame generation option on the Frame generation page is now labelled "CSR Flux BETA". CSR 1.3 stays the default.

Reliability fix for CSR Flux BETA. It no longer quietly switches to CSR 2.0.

Also in this release, from Beta3:

New app layout. A redesigned main window: a home page that shows what's running at a glance, and separate pages for Upscaling, Frame generation, Games, CPU, Advanced and About.

CSR Flux BETA. A new AI frame generation option in the Frame generation menu, next to CSR 1.3 and CSR 2.0. CSR 1.3 stays the default.

CSR AI (experimental) upscaler. A new AI upscaling option in the Upscaling menu. It's experimental; CSR 1.3 stays the default.

Fix for CSR 2.0 low-latency mode. With low-latency mode or the Competitive preset on, CSR 2.0 now adds generated frames as it should.

And from Beta2:

Anti-cheat safe mode. FrameFX recognizes games protected by anti-cheat (EasyAntiCheat, BattlEye, Riot Vanguard, FACEIT, Ricochet and others) and keeps itself in a safe state for them: it only captures the game's window, never touches the game, and leaves out CPU options that would change the game's priority or cores. "Anti-cheat safe mode" shows next to the game and in the overlay, and the Games page has the list of protected games, where you can add your own or mark a game as not protected.

CSR 2.0 upscaler. A new CSR 2.0 option in the Upscaling menu next to CSR 1.3, with the same presets and sharpness slider. CSR 1.3 stays the default.

Steam Deck. SteamOS Preview 2 brings CSR 2.0 frame generation and a CSR 2.0 upscaler option to Desktop Mode.

And from Beta1:

CSR 2.0 in the frame generation menu. Settings → Frame generation now lists Off, CSR 1.3, CSR 2.0 and AMD FSR 3, with CSR 2.0's own options shown right below it. CSR 1.3 stays the default.

Up to 20×. With CSR 2.0 you can pick 2× up to 20×. Output never goes above your refresh rate, so the high multipliers are for high-refresh displays (20× is meant for 360 Hz and up).

Smoother pacing, sharper HUD and cursor. Generated frames are timed to your display, and your HUD and cursor stay crisp on them.

In both the Windows and ROG Ally / Ally X editions. Includes Beta7, Beta6 and Beta5: the CPU tab and ray-traced lighting.

CPU tab. High-performance power mode, priority and the fastest cores for the game, with background apps you choose kept out of the way. Off by default, undone when the game closes, with a quick before/after benchmark.

Ray-traced lighting. Brighter and more natural, and it runs lighter on GTX 1050 Ti-class cards.

Separate ROG Ally / Ally X and Steam Deck editions are available. The Steam Deck tab is a native SteamOS preview (Desktop Mode).

Beta4 added per-game profiles, Ctrl+Alt+C compare and a steadier SSGI option.

Features

Frame generation up to 4×

Choose 2×, 3× or 4×, or 8× if you want it, and up to 20× with CSR 2.0; it never goes above your refresh rate, and it backs off if the game would slow down.

Upscaling to 1440p or 4K

AMD FSR 1–4, Intel XeSS, or CSR (Chopsticks Super Resolution), our own; FSR 4 needs a Radeon RX 9000.

Works on any window

Pick a game or app and press Start; nothing in the game changes.

Click-through overlay

It sits exactly over the window and follows it; Ctrl+Alt+F hides it, Ctrl+Alt+Q stops it, and Ctrl+Alt+C toggles it to compare.

Competitive mode

One click for the most fps and the least delay, with the latency budget on by default.

Per-game profiles

Each game remembers its preset, frame generation, lighting, upscaler and output resolution.

CPU tab

High-performance power mode, priority and the fastest cores for the game, with background apps you choose kept out of the way; off by default, undone when the game closes, with a quick before/after benchmark.

Ray-traced lighting, experimental

Extra light, reflections and contact shadows, even on a GTX 1050 Ti or 980 Ti, and off by default.

Games only

It runs only while a game is in front, and pauses when you alt-tab.

Prefers the fast GPU

On a laptop it picks the dedicated GPU, or you choose which one.

Automatic updates

It checks, verifies each download, and rolls back if an update doesn't start.
